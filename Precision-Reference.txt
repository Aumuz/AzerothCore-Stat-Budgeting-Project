===========================================================================================================================================
  Introduction and Workflow:
===========================================================================================================================================

      The purpose of this document is to provide readers with a concise framework for the verification of stat budgets
  of existing equipment and weapon items in AzerothCore. Using this framework, readers can observe and validate how
  stat budgets vary by ItemLevel (iLvl), Quality, and item configuration. This document also defines the procedure for
  calculating item buy and sell prices based on the Final Budget.

      This document is intended for reference and verification. It is not optimized for day-to-day item creation. Developers
  seeking guidance on creating custom items should refer to "A Developers Handbook to Stat Budgeting Custom Items in
  AzerothCore v1.0" (./Developer-Handbook.txt).



  This guide is organized into a four-step workflow. Each step builds directly on the previous step.

   - Step 1: Determine the Base Budget from the "Observable ItemLevel to Budget Table".
   - Step 2: Calculate the Final Budget by applying the appropriate Slot Multiplier.
   - Step 3: Allocate the Final Budget using the "Observable Stat to Cost Table".
   - Step 4: Use the Final Budget to calculate the item's buy and sell price.

===========================================================================================================================================
  Step 3: Allocate the Final Budget using the "Observable Stat to Cost Table".
===========================================================================================================================================

      The Final Budget is allocated by assigning stats according to their respective budget costs. The following formulas
  define how DPS, Armor, and stat values consume budget. The total cost of all assigned values must not exceed the Final
  Budget.



    DPS Cost   = (dmg_min1 + dmg_max1) / 2 / (delay / 1000)
    Armor Cost = armor * 0.20
    Stat Cost  = stat_value * Cost

  Socket Bonus Cost:

      Socket bonuses consume budget at 50% of the cost of the corresponding stat. To calculate the cost of a socket bonus,
  determine the stat cost using the Stat to Cost Table, then multiply that value by 0.50.

      Example:
        ITEM_MOD_STRENGTH cost = 0.85
        Socket bonus: +2 Strength
        Socket bonus cost = 2 * (0.85 * 0.50) = 0.85



  Random Property and Random Suffix Cost:

      Items with a Random Property or Random Suffix consume budget equal to 35% of the Final Budget. This cost is applied
  once per item and is independent of the specific stats granted by the random effect.

  Step 4: Use the Final Budget to calculate the item's buy and sell price.
===========================================================================================================================================

      An item's SellPrice (in copper) is derived from its Final Budget and ItemLevel using the ItemLevel Multiplier defined below.

  BuyPrice  = SellPrice * 5

  Additional Information:
===========================================================================================================================================

      This document defines a complete workflow for verifying stat budgets and item prices for existing equipment and weapon
  items in AzerothCore. All values presented are derived from observed items only; no interpolation or synthesized values were
  used to fill unobserved ItemLevel or Quality ranges.

      Readers should expect individual items to deviate from the values presented in this document. The budgets defined
  here represent the average observed power level for a given ItemLevel and Quality Tier, not a strict specification applied
  uniformly across all items. Blizzard itemization exhibits intentional variance driven by gameplay considerations, class
  identity, encounter design, and progression pacing. As a result, deviation from the average is not an anomaly but a
  structural characteristic of the itemization system being modeled.

      This document therefore describes the expected power level of items within AzerothCore, providing a baseline against
  which existing items can be evaluated rather than a set of exact targets every item is required to match.
