===========================================================================================================================================
  A Derivation and Validation of Stat Budgeting in AzerothCore. v1.0
===========================================================================================================================================



===========================================================================================================================================
  Introduction and Scope:
===========================================================================================================================================

      The AzerothCore Stat Budgeting Project provides a reproducible framework for creating custom equipment and weapons that conform to
  the intended power progression of Blizzard items in World of Warcraft 3.3.5. When used as a complete set, the documents in this project
  allow developers to produce items that are internally consistent with existing game data while remaining flexible enough for custom design.

      This document explains how the project's values and recommendations were derived. It describes the data sources used, the constraints
  applied during collection, the methods used to derive and validate stat budget curves, the rules governing interpolation, and the process
  by which supporting tables were constructed.

      This document may be read either before or after the accompanying developer-facing guides. Its purpose is not to instruct item
  creation directly, but to establish why the recommendations provided elsewhere are reliable.



===========================================================================================================================================
  Foundational Assumptions and Constraints:
===========================================================================================================================================

      This project is built on the premise that an item's effective power in World of Warcraft 3.3.5 is fully expressed through three
  components: armor, DPS, and allocated stats. In existing game data, these components are governed primarily by an item's Quality
  and Item Level (iLvl).

      From this premise, the project assumes that a total stat budget can be derived for any item defined by a given Quality and Item Level.
  This derivation requires that item slots-defined by class, subclass, and InventoryType, apply consistent multipliers to that budget,
  and that armor, DPS, and allocated stats each consume predictable portions of it.

      Under these constraints, armor values, DPS values, and stat distribution patterns are treated as derivable functions of Quality
  and Item Level rather than independent design choices.



===========================================================================================================================================
  Data Sources and Collection Constraints:
===========================================================================================================================================

      All calculations in the AzerothCore Stat Budget Project are derived from the AzerothCore database (ACDB 335.14-dev). Item metadata
  was copied from table: item_template to create the project's dataset.

  The following filters were applied to isolate player-obtainable items that conform to standard 3.3.5 itemization rules.

    Item Inclusion Constraints:

     - class = 2 AND subclass NOT IN (9, 11, 12, 14, 20) - Include Weapons except Obsolete, Exotic, Miscellaneous, and Fishing Pole.
                                                           These items do no conform to standard itemization.

     - class = 4 AND InventoryType NOT IN (0, 4, 12, 18, 19, 24, 27, 28) - Include Equipment except Non-Equipable, Shirt, Trinket, Bag,
                                                                           Tabard, Ammo, Quiver, and Relic. These items typically contain
                                                                           no stats, or are outside the scope of this project.

     - Quality IN (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) - This excludes Poor, Heirloom, and Artifact items, as these items do not conform to standard itemization.

     - ItemLevel > 5 - This excludes starting gear, and non-standard items, such as Event, GM and Test items (typically ItemLevel = 0).

     - RequiredLevel > 5 - This excludes starting gear, and non-standard items, such as Event, GM and Test items (typically RequiredLevel = 0).



  Name, Description, and Accessibility Filters:

   - name and description fields excluding values containing "DEPRECATED", "MONSTER", "PLACEHOLDER", or "TEST". This excludes items unobtainable by players.



  Structural Sanity Checks:

    - Weapons with no dmg_min1 and no dmg_max1 values were removed.

    - Equipment with no armor and no stat values were removed.

    - Impossible item combinations (such as 1H Axe, Two Handed) were also removed.

    - During manual review of Legendary-quality items, five items were removed from the dataset due to known player unavailability,
      including items never obtainable in-game and one historically limited drop later rendered non-functional.



  Resulting Dataset Size:

   - These filters resulted in a final data set of 18318 items (3263 weapons, 15055 equipment).



  Acquisition Normalization: (limited use)

    - After the creation of the initial dataset, items were cross-referenced to determine drop location (dungeon, raid, world),
      drop source (creature loot, quest rewards, and etc.), drop rate, and drop cadence (how often an item can drop within seven days,
      given drop rate, respawn time, and spawn count).

    - This information was used to determine an item's availability weight. This weight was only used to generate the
      "Recommended ItemLevel and Quality by Player Level" in the Developer Recommendations.

    - This weighting was not applied to any budget curves, DPS calculations, armor values, stat counts, or stat distribution tables.



===========================================================================================================================================
  Initial Budget Model Definition:
===========================================================================================================================================

      The initial budget model defines an item's budget as the sum of its observable armor, DPS, stat, and socket costs, normalized by
  slot-specific multipliers to allow comparison across item slots.

      Costs were derived from observed armor, DPS, and stat values using a unified budget scale, where 1 point of Stamina represents a full
  budget unit and DPS consumes budget on a 1-to-1 basis. Socket bonuses were treated as consuming half the equivalent stat cost.

      Initial slot multipliers were introduced as neutral scaling factors to normalize budget consumption across item slots. At the outset
  of curve construction, slot multipliers were treated as provisional normalization factors rather than fixed constants. The initial values
  served only to allow curve construction to proceed and were not considered authoritative until validated against observed item distributions.

      Actual Budget is defined as the sum of Armor Cost, DPS Cost, Total Stat Cost, and Socket Cost. Armor Cost is calculated as armor x 0.20.
  DPS Cost is calculated as (dmg_min1 + dmg_max1) / 2 / (delay / 1000). Total Stat Cost is the sum of each stat_value multiplied by its
  corresponding stat cost. Socket bonuses consume 50% of the equivalent stat cost.

      Normalized Budget is calculated by dividing Actual Budget by the item's slot multiplier, allowing budgets from different item slots to
  be compared on a common scale. Items with a random property or random suffix were further divided by 0.65, reflecting an observed fixed
  budget cost of 35%.

      Once normalized budgets were calculated, items were grouped by Quality and ItemLevel. Crude curves were then derived for each Quality
  by finding the average normalized budget of each group, then ordering the results by ItemLevel. Each curve was further refined by applying
  a 3 point smoothing window, followed by monotonic enforcement. The result was a smooth, monotonically increasing curve for each Quality tier.



===========================================================================================================================================
  Curve Validation and Adjustment:
===========================================================================================================================================

      After initial curve construction, validation and adjustment were performed to ensure alignment between observed item budgets and
  curve-derived target budgets.

      For each item in the dataset, the Normalized Budget was compared against its corresponding Quality and ItemLevel target value.
  Both absolute deviation and budget ratio were recorded.



  A series of evaluation reports were generated by grouping items by:

   - Quality

   - ItemLevel

   - Slot

   - A global aggregate including all items

  These reports were used to assess systemic deviation patterns rather than individual outliers.



      Slot multipliers were adjusted using the Slot report by recalculating each multiplier based on the average budget ratio for that slot,
  according to the following rule:

  New Multiplier = Old Multiplier × Budget Ratio



  After updating slot multipliers, budget curves were regenerated and the validation process repeated.



  Validation Summary (Final Iteration)

   - Global ratio: 98.04%

   - Quality-level ratios: 96.74% - 102.17%

   - ItemLevel alignment: 81% of ItemLevels within ±10% of target budget

   - Slot alignment: All slots within ±4% of target budget



      The final values reported in this project were selected from the iteration that best balanced accuracy across all validation reports,
  rather than the iteration that minimized error in any single dimension.

  Slot multiplier adjustments were evaluated holistically, as changes to individual slots were observed to propagate variance across other
  validation dimensions.

      Incomplete curves reflect Blizzard's itemization practices rather than missing data; not every ItemLevel–Quality combination exists
  in the dataset. Among all qualities, the Epic curve was the most complete and was therefore selected as the reference curve for interpolation.

      Three slope regions with internally consistent growth rates were identified within the Epic curve: ItemLevels 10–60, 70–140,
  and 150–300. For each region, quality-specific ratios relative to Epic were calculated and used to reconstruct missing values in
  other quality curves.

      Where data was absent across an entire region, constrained fallbacks were applied. The Common quality curve lacked data in the
  150–300 range and therefore reused the 70–140 slope region. Legendary lacked data in the 10–60 range and similarly reused the 70–140 region.

      Once all ItemLevel brackets were populated, monotonic enforcement was applied within each quality curve. Additional monotonic constraints
  were enforced across qualities to ensure expected ordering (Legendary ≥ Epic ≥ Rare ≥ Uncommon ≥ Common).

      Poor-quality items (Quality = 0) were not present in the dataset but may be required for custom content. These values were extrapolated
  from the Common curve using fixed multipliers: 75% for ItemLevels 10–60, 70% for 70–140, and 65% for 150–300.



===========================================================================================================================================
  Derivation of Supporting Tables:
===========================================================================================================================================

      The bulk of analysis in the AzerothCore Stat Budgeting Project was concerned with the production and validation of the budget model
  (cost, curves, and multipliers). However, additional context was required for developers to produce BlizzLike items.

  Studies have been conducted on the following topics:

   - 1. Recommended Quality and ItemLevel based on player level.
   - 2. Recommended DPS values based on ItemLevel.
   - 3. Recommended armor values based on ItemLevel and Armor Type.
   - 4. Recommended number of stats based on Quality and ItemLevel.
   - 5. Recommended distribution of stat values.
   - 6. Recommended buy/sell price.

  Slot Group was heavily utilized within the recommendation document. These groups are classified by items of similar game-play roles and budget behavior.



  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Recommended Quality and ItemLevel based on player level.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

         To generate this table, dataset items were first grouped by Slot Group, RequiredLevel (in 10 level brackets) and Quality or ItemLevel.
     Item counts for each group were adjusted using the weights derived in data collection. As a result, each item contributed a weighted
     value between 0.20 and 1.00. The most common group (highest count) was saved as the recommendation. Finally, as Quality and ItemLevel
     were calculated separately, these were combined into a final table.



  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  2. Recommended DPS values based on ItemLevel.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

         This series of tables were generated by grouping weapon items by Slot Group, ItemLevel (in 10 level brackets), and
     InventoryType (to define handedness).

         For each group, an average dmg_min1, dmg_max1, delay, and Actual Budget was determined. 3 point smoothing was then applied. Monotonic
     progression was also enforced on all columns excluding delay (which did not display monotonic progression).

         dmg_min1, dmg_max1, and delay were rounded to the nearest whole number, as these are saved as integers in the database. Using these
     values, DPS was calculated, and rounded to two decimal points.

     A DPS to Actual Budget ratios was calculated for each group, using the smoothed average budget, and rounded DPS values.

         Once all information was collected and calculated, the information was divided into eight tables, representing Slot Group,
     and handedness (when applicable).



  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  3. Recommended armor values based on ItemLevel and Armor Type.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

         This series of tables were generated by grouping equipment (armor) items by Slot Group, ItemLevel and subclass (to define armor type).
     Items without an armor value were excluded.

         For each group an average of armor and Actual Budget was determined. 3 point smoothing and monotonic progression was then applied,
     and armor was rounded to the nearest whole number.

     Armor Cost was calculated (armor x 0.20), and an Armor Cost to Actual Budget ratio was determined.

         The information collected was then divided into six tables. Four tables depicting Slot Groups Major-Armor, Moderate-Armor, and
     Minor-Armor, divided into each armor type, Cloth, Leather, Mail, and Plate. Two tables depict Shield and Back (Cloak). The Accessory
     Slot Group was excluded, as these items do not contain consistent armor values.



  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  4. Recommended number of stats based on Quality and ItemLevel.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

         To generate these three tables, items were grouped by Quality, ItemLevel (in 20 level brackets) and Slot Group. Stats were then
     aggregated and counted within each group. Any non-zero stat_value which matched a non-zero stat_type, were counted as 1. These counts
     were totaled, and an average count was determined per group. This average count was then smoothed and monotonic progression was enforced.
     Finally, the number was rounded to the nearest whole value.

     The result was three Quality tables, divided by ItemLevel and Slot Group.

         Common items within the dataset did not return any stats beyond DPS or Armor, and have been excluded. Legendary items were also
     excluded due to the small sample size. Poor items, while generally excluded from this project, were confirmed to also contain no stats
     beyond DPS or Armor (via MYSQL query on item_template).



  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  5. Recommended distribution of stat values.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

         Observation of stat distribution within the dataset show consistency between Quality and ItemLevel. Slot Group is the only marked
     difference in how stats are distributed.

         The Stat Distribution Table was generated by grouping dataset items by Slot Group. Stats were then aggregated according to their
     stat_type/stat_value combination, and grouped by Stat Group (stats with similar game-play roles and costs). The ratio of each Stat Group
     to the total sum of stats, was then calculated for each Slot Group.

     This table omits armor and DPS from ratio calculations.



  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  6. Recommended buy/sell price.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

         Buy and sell pricing was analyzed separately from stat budgeting, as price is not directly encoded as a stat cost but reflects
     item value within the economy.

         Only items containing both BuyPrice and SellPrice were included in this analysis. Of the 18,318 items in the dataset, 13,096 items
     (71.49%) met this criterion. Items lacking either value were excluded.

         Analysis of these items showed that SellPrice is consistently equal to 20% of BuyPrice across all Qualities, ItemLevels, and
     Slot Groups. This establishes a fixed relationship:

     SellPrice = BuyPrice × 0.20
     BuyPrice = SellPrice × 5

         Further analysis showed that SellPrice does not scale directly with raw stat budget alone. Instead, SellPrice is proportional to
     the product of Actual Budget and an ItemLevel-specific multiplier.

         To derive this multiplier, items were grouped by ItemLevel bracket and the median ratio of Actual Budget to SellPrice was calculated.
     This ratio was rounded to the nearest whole number and recorded as the ItemLevel Multiplier.

     Recommended BuyPrice and SellPrice values are therefore derived using:

     SellPrice = Actual Budget × ItemLevel Multiplier
     BuyPrice = SellPrice × 5

     This approach reflects observed Blizzard pricing behavior and preserves economic consistency across ItemLevels.



===========================================================================================================================================
  Scope Limits, Intended Use, and Related Documents:
===========================================================================================================================================

      The AzerothCore Stat Budgeting Project defines a data-driven model for item stat budgeting in World of Warcraft 3.3.5, based on
  observed AzerothCore itemization patterns. It is intended for developers creating custom weapons and equipment that conform to established
  power progression defined by ItemLevel and Quality.

      This project is not intended to prescribe optimal or competitive itemization strategies, nor to replicate Blizzard's internal design
  process. Instead, it provides a consistent, verifiable framework for producing BlizzLike items that integrate cleanly into existing
  progression systems. While the model is calibrated to typical Wrath of the Lich King itemization, it is sufficiently flexible to support
  experimental or best-in-slot designs at the developer's discretion.



  The documents below provide additional context and guidance, depending on development needs.
